## 🎯 Module Overview

This module enabled me to:

- Develop a substantial individual computing project that demonstrates the ability to conduct independent inquiry and manage a complex research timeline effectively.
- Identify and apply appropriate research methodologies and data collection techniques to address a specific research question within the computing discipline.
- Critically appraise academic literature to establish a research space and justify the project's relevance through a structured proposal and ethical approval process.
- Demonstrate professional competencies in project management, risk assessment, and the communication of technical findings through a written dissertation and artefact presentation.

## Unit 1: An Introduction to Your Dissertation

**Key Concepts:**
- The dissertation as a continuation of Research Methods and Professional Practice
- Distinction between primary and secondary research
- John Swales' CARS Model (Create a Research Space)
- Developing research questions, aims and objectives
- Structure and importance of the research proposal

**Reflections:**
The starting point was Swales' CARS model, which asks where a study's research space sits within existing work. When I applied it to my own topic, I realised that my initial idea of "measuring technical debt in trading systems" was a problem statement rather than a research question. Almost every technical debt tool I knew analyses source code, yet in my own environment most critical systems are vendor products whose code we never see. Naming that gap made the project researchable: can debt be prioritised from operational data alone? Turning this into separate research questions on prioritisation, proxy validity and ranking stability gave me a structure I relied on until the final chapter. Next time I would write the research questions down even earlier, because so many later decisions depended on them.

## Unit 3: Project Types and Research Methods

**Key Concepts:**
- Overview of computing project types including software development and empirical analytical
- Differences between quantitative and qualitative methods
- Approaches to data collection and analysis
- Designing a research methodology to meet objectives
- Determining sample sizes for statistical power

**Reflections:**
This unit shaped the decision that defined my dissertation: building and evaluating an artefact under Design Science instead of running a survey. Because I wanted to show that a quantitative framework works on real operational data, an empirical-analytical design using existing ITSM and CMDB records fitted better than collecting opinions. The sample size discussion was uncomfortable. With only six services that had complete data, I knew early on that formal rank-correlation tests would have limited statistical power. Rather than hiding that, I designed the evaluation around robustness instead: weight sensitivity, comparison with alternative methods and removal of services. Looking back, accepting this limitation early was more productive than trying to engineer it away.

## Unit 4: Reviewing Literature

**Key Concepts:**
- Purpose and structure of a literature review
- Critical analysis and evaluation of academic sources
- Identifying gaps in current research
- Avoidance of self plagiarism from previous modules
- Conducting the first supervisory meeting

**Reflections:**
Reviewing the literature confirmed the gap I had suspected and sharpened it. Most technical debt research measures debt in code, and the literature on operational metrics such as DORA rarely treats them as debt indicators. The useful part of the review was finding studies that link individual operational metrics to specific debt dimensions, which let me justify each of my five indicators from existing evidence rather than intuition. The warning about self-plagiarism also mattered, because parts of the topic came from my Research Methods literature review. I rewrote that material from scratch around the new research questions instead of adapting it. My first supervisor meeting pushed me to narrow the scope, which was the right call. <!-- CHECK: both sentences are assumptions; correct them to what actually happened. -->

## Unit 5: Research Ethics

**Key Concepts:**
- Moral principles governing the conduct of researchers
- Developing accountable and trustworthy research
- Key ethical components including informed consent and confidentiality
- The right of participants to withdraw from the study
- The process for obtaining ethical approval

**Reflections:**
Ethics turned out to be less about participants and more about data, because my study used no interviews or surveys. The real questions were confidentiality and responsible use. I was working with production records from my own employer, a regulated Swiss bank, so before any data left the organisation I anonymised all service, team and vendor identifiers, exported only the fields the indicators needed and agreed the scope of use with the Head of Trading IT. The issue I had not anticipated was misuse of the output. A debt ranking can easily be read as a ranking of teams, even though high-debt services usually reflect historical decisions rather than the current team. Making that boundary explicit, both in the agreement and in the thesis, is something I will carry into future work with metrics.

## Units 7-30: Dissertation

**Key Concepts:**
- Execution of the research project and dissertation writing
- Regular supervisory meetings including a compulsory session in Week 9
- Preparation for the artefact presentation at the online conference
- Double marking process involving two independent markers
- Potential for publishing research with the supervisor as co-author

**Reflections:**
The execution phase taught me that the hardest part of the research was not the computation but the definitions. I expected implementing TOPSIS and running 20,000 Monte Carlo weightings to be the main challenge. Instead, deciding what an incident count or a derived change failure rate can legitimately mean took far more effort than the model. I also expected the comparison with SAW and VIKOR to simply confirm my results. Instead, the methods disagreed about the top-ranked service in the 2024 data, and that disagreement became one of the most instructive findings, because it forced me to take a position on whether one debt dimension can offset another. Design Science helped by keeping me honest about the difference between what the artefact demonstrates and what it only suggests. The regular supervisor meetings were where most of these judgements were tested. <!-- CHECK: add a concrete example from a supervisor meeting if you have one. --> For future work, the clear next steps are external validation at other institutions and a longitudinal study. I would also like to pursue publication with my supervisor, as the module encourages.
